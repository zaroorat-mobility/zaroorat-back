## BD-1 — Insufficient wallet balance at completion · **REVERSED: no cap, no partial deduction, no shortfall**

**This reverses BD-1 as it stood after the previous revision.** Every prior version of BD-1 (reservation-era, and the two "cap deduction, record shortfall" revisions that followed it) is **withdrawn**, per your explicit instruction: _"REMOVE the old business rule that says: 'If commission is greater than current wallet balance, deduct whatever is available and record the remaining amount as a shortfall.' This is NOT part of the new business model."_ There is no cap. There is no partial deduction. There is no shortfall record. The `expectedAmount` field this mechanism previously wrote to (`data-model.md` §2.3) is removed along with it — see `data-model.md` for the corresponding schema withdrawal.

**Why a shortfall was ever possible, and why that's now treated differently**: under the prior design, "cap and record a shortfall" was the _normal_, expected handling for a real (if rare) situation — the wallet balance legitimately could differ between acceptance and completion (an admin adjustment, a refund). Your instruction reclassifies this: the system's designed invariant is `wallet.balance >= ride.commissionAmount` from the moment of acceptance onward, and — per your instruction — _"The system does not have another normal feature that deducts money from this driver commission wallet during an active ride."_ Given that, a wallet balance insufficient to cover `ride.commissionAmount` at completion is not a normal, expected outcome of ordinary operation; it is a sign that the invariant was violated by something outside the ride's own lifecycle (most plausibly a staff `ADMIN_DEBIT` adjustment, or a Commission-Wallet-recharge refund taken after acceptance — both still legitimate operations in their own right, just not accounted for by this invariant). The correction is not to silently absorb that as routine (capping) but to treat it as **exceptional**.

**Resolved terms**:

- At ride completion (commission-model rides only — FR-024c), the wallet row is locked (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) and `currentBalance` is read from that locked row.
- **If `currentBalance >= ride.commissionAmount`** (the expected, normal case — guaranteed by the accept-time check and the absence of any other normal wallet-spending feature during an active ride): deduct **exactly** `ride.commissionAmount`. Not `min(...)`, not a capped amount — the full stored figure, always, in this case. Write the `RIDE_COMMISSION` transaction (`amount = -ride.commissionAmount`, `balanceAfter = currentBalance - ride.commissionAmount`) and the matching balanced ledger group, atomically with the ride's completion.
- **If `currentBalance < ride.commissionAmount`** (the invariant-violation case): this is treated as an **exceptional condition, not a normal financial outcome**, per your instruction's explicit framing. The system MUST NOT:
  - deduct the available (insufficient) balance as if it were the full commission (no `min()`, no partial deduction);
  - write a `RIDE_COMMISSION` transaction claiming the commission was collected (no false "full commission collected" record);
  - invent a debt, receivable, or "shortfall balance" concept for the driver (none exists in this system for this wallet, and your instruction explicitly forbids introducing one here).

  Instead: **no wallet transaction is written and no ledger entry is posted for this ride's commission** (nothing partial, nothing false — the state before this step and after it, with respect to the wallet, is identical: consistency preserved by writing nothing). The condition is recorded for investigation/reconciliation using this codebase's **existing** observability mechanisms — a structured error-level log entry (via the existing `pino` logger, unchanged redaction rules), a domain metric counter (`commission_deduction_invariant_violation_total`, following the existing `incrementCounter` pattern already used for every other money-affecting operation, constitution §17.1), and a durable outbox event (`driver.commission_wallet.collection_failed`, carrying `driverId`, `rideId`, `ride.commissionAmount`, and the actual balance found) so staff/ops tooling can be notified and investigate — **not** a new business/financial entity, table, or workflow. No automatic retry and no collection mechanism is introduced; this differs deliberately from the _customer_-side receivable/retry pattern elsewhere in this codebase (`research.md` §1.5/§2.C), which does not apply here because your instruction restricts any debt/receivable-shaped mechanism to cases "an existing approved business requirement explicitly requires," and none does for this wallet.

- **The rest of ride completion is unaffected.** The ride still transitions to `COMPLETED`, `RideFare` is still created, and the customer-facing side of the ride is entirely unaffected by a commission-collection invariant violation — nothing in your instruction asks for the ride itself to fail, only for the _commission-deduction financial operation_ to fail safely rather than partially succeed. See `plan.md` "Ride Completion" for exactly how this is sequenced inside the existing `completeRide` transaction without requiring the whole transaction to roll back over a driver-wallet anomaly.
- **Negative or zero determined commission** (unrelated to the above — a property of the _stored_ amount itself, decided once at acceptance, not of a balance shortfall): if `ride.commissionAmount <= 0` (possible on a heavily-discounted ride, per `PricingService`'s own documented policy that the platform absorbs a discount exceeding its margin — `pricing.service.ts` lines 305-314), the amount actually deducted is `max(0, ride.commissionAmount)`, i.e. exactly zero. This is not a partial deduction of a positive stored amount — it is the exact (zero) stored amount, deducted in full, and is unaffected by this decision's reversal (FR-024a1 is unchanged).
- **Consequence**: the Commission Wallet can still never go negative through the ride-commission path — not because a cap prevents it (there is no cap), but because the deduction now simply **does not occur** whenever it would drive the balance negative. Only an authorized administrative adjustment or a post-recharge refund can still produce a negative balance (spec.md Edge Cases), unaffected by this decision.

## BD-2 — Subscription expiry mid-ride · **Unchanged**

Carried forward verbatim from the original decision: subscription status is evaluated only at new-ride offer/acceptance time. A ride already accepted before expiry completes exactly as it would for a driver with an active subscription. This decision now additionally interacts with `Ride.driverPaymentModel`: since that ride's model was pinned `SUBSCRIPTION` at acceptance (data-model.md §3.3), completion correctly skips all Commission Wallet logic regardless of the subscription's live status at completion time — the pinning mechanism reinforces, rather than changes, this decision.

## BD-3 — Plan change and cancellation timing · **Unchanged**

Carried forward verbatim: a driver-initiated plan change or cancellation is recorded immediately but takes effect only when the current, already-paid-for period ends. No proration, no partial refund. ## BD-4 — Reservation/freeze mechanism · **REMOVED**

**This reverses a decision the original design made** (the original spec required a reservation at accept time, released/consumed at completion). The instruction driving this revision states the reversal directly and also requires the reasoning to be spelled out, so it is recorded here as a decision even though — unlike BD-1/BD-2/BD-3 — it was not put to the product owner as an open question, because the instruction left no ambiguity about the desired outcome ("Do NOT introduce reserved_balance / locked_balance unless there is another clearly justified business requirement... Clearly explain... why reservation/freeze is not required").

**Full reasoning**: reproduced in `revision-002-dual-model.md` §10; summarized here:

1. Reservation existed to prevent two simultaneous ride-accepts for the same driver from jointly over-committing the wallet before either commits.
2. This platform already enforces, independently of this feature, that a driver can have at most one active ride at a time — and this is **database-enforced, not merely an application-level check**: `LifecycleService.acceptRideRequest` calls `rideRepo.findActiveByDriver` (`src/modules/rides/repositories/ride.repository.ts:90-101`, a plain unlocked `findFirst`) as the application-level fast path, but the actual guarantee against two different ride requests racing to be accepted by the same driver is a **partial unique index**, `rides_active_driver_key ON rides (driver_id) WHERE status IN ('ACCEPTED','DRIVER_ARRIVING','DRIVER_ARRIVED','IN_PROGRESS')` — added in migration `20260821130000_ride_active_uniqueness`, whose own header comment states exactly this reasoning verbatim: _"Two different requests accepted by the same driver ... in the same instant could both still pass the application check before either commits"_ and that the index is the intended DB-level backstop. Under this confirmed constraint, the scenario reservation was defending against cannot occur — a second racing accept for the same driver fails the unique-index insert, not merely "shouldn't happen in practice."
3. The only wallet-mutating operation in the ride flow is the completion-time deduction, which happens at most once per ride, and at most one ride is ever in flight per driver — so there is structurally at most one pending deduction per driver at a time.
4. The deduction is still protected by a database row lock (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) at the moment of the write — this is ordinary write-safety (constitution §5.1), not a race-arbitration mechanism, and it also means that even in a hypothetical bug elsewhere that let two rides be simultaneously active for one driver, the wallet itself could never be corrupted or driven incorrectly negative: each completion reads the _current_ balance under lock and caps its own deduction against it (BD-1), so a second completion simply finds a smaller or zero balance rather than double-spending.
5. Duplicate/retried completion for the _same_ ride was never something reservation addressed anyway — that is handled by the unique-index-backed idempotency check (data-model.md §4.2), unchanged.

**Removed as a consequence**: `DriverCommissionWallet.lockedBalance`, the `WalletHold` reuse, the `COMMISSION_RESERVATION`/`RESERVATION_RELEASE` transaction types, the "at most one active reservation per ride" constraint, and the `DRIVER_COMMISSION_WALLET_RESERVATION_TTL_SEC` safety-net job (nothing can be orphaned if nothing is ever reserved).

## BD-5 — Payment-model switching timing and per-ride pinning · **Resolved directly from instructions**

The driving instruction states the governing principles explicitly enough that this did not need to be escalated as an open question: _"Define when the new model becomes effective... Do not allow one ride to be charged using two different models... The payment model applicable to the ride must be determined consistently by the backend... Preserve existing no-proration/no-mid-period-change rules if they remain valid."_

**Resolved terms**:

- **`COMMISSION → SUBSCRIPTION`**: takes effect immediately, at the moment the new subscription activates (payment confirmed). Rationale, applying the instruction's own logic: a commission wallet balance is not a time-boxed, already-paid-for period the way a subscription is — there is nothing to "waste" by switching immediately, so the no-proration/no-mid-period principle (BD-3) doesn't apply here; it only applies where a period was already paid for.
- **`SUBSCRIPTION → COMMISSION`**: if the driver has a currently `ACTIVE` subscription, the switch is staged (`Driver.pendingPaymentModel = 'COMMISSION'`) and applied only when that subscription's current period ends — directly reusing BD-3's "no proration, current paid period runs its course" principle, generalized from a plan-to-plan change to a model-to-model change. If the driver has no currently active subscription, the switch applies immediately (there is no paid-for period being preserved).
- **Per-ride pinning**: `Ride.driverPaymentModel` is set once, from `Driver.paymentModel`'s value at the exact moment of acceptance, and is the only value ride completion consults for commission-handling purposes — never the driver's live model. This is the direct, structural implementation of "do not allow one ride to be charged using two different models," and it is why a mid-ride model switch (in either direction) never needs a special case at completion: completion simply reads what was already recorded.

**Configuration/mechanism reused, not invented**: this staging pattern is the exact template `DriverSubscription.pendingPlanId` already established for plan changes (BD-3) — a new decision in business terms, but not a new mechanism in implementation terms (constitution §1.5/§6.4's "no second mechanism for a solved problem").

## BD-6 — Exact Commission Plan definition · **Resolved from existing code**

**Correction to the prior revision**: the earlier design in this file/`data-model.md` proposed a new, standalone `CommissionPlan` table ("a company-defined description of how commission is calculated... a record/label of the rate the existing `PricingService` already computes"). Having now traced the actual calculation (`src/modules/pricing/services/pricing.service.ts`, `PricingRuleRepository`), that proposal is **withdrawn** — the codebase already has a complete, versioned, per-ride-pinned commission-plan mechanism, and introducing a second one would be exactly the "completely new calculation system" your instruction forbids. `CommissionPlan` as a distinct entity is **removed from the data model** (see `data-model.md` §2.5 for the corresponding schema withdrawal). "Commission Plan," for the purposes of this feature, **is** the existing `PricingRule` — specifically its `commissionRatePct` field and the calculation that consumes it.

## BD-7 — Commission is determined once, at acceptance, and never recalculated · **Resolved directly from instructions**

This is resolved directly from your instructions, not escalated as an open question, for the same reason BD-4/BD-5 were: the instruction states the required outcome unambiguously ("THE COMMISSION IS DETERMINED ONCE WHEN THE RIDE IS ACCEPTED... There is NO second commission calculation at ride completion"), leaving no reasonable alternative reading. It is recorded as a decision because it **reverses part of the immediately-prior revision of this specification**, which had described an accept-time "expected commission" (an estimate) and a completion-time "final commission" (a fresh calculation from the ride's actual measured fare) as two distinct values. That two-value design is withdrawn.

**Resolved terms**:

- At ride acceptance, for a `COMMISSION`-model ride only, the backend determines the commission amount applicable to that ride — using the existing Commission Plan mechanism (BD-6, the pinned `PricingRule.commissionRatePct`) applied to whatever ride/fare inputs are available at acceptance (the ride's quoted/requested fare inputs — the same inputs `RideRequest.quotedFare` is already derived from, per the existing, unmodified quote-time pricing path) — and persists it as `Ride.commissionAmount` (`data-model.md` §3.3), in the same write that creates the `Ride` row.
- This determined amount is what the accept-time eligibility check compares the wallet balance against (FR-015), and it is the **only** figure ride completion ever reads for this purpose (FR-020). No commission-calculation function of any kind runs during ride completion.
- **The ride's customer-facing final fare remains free to be determined/confirmed at completion**, exactly as it already is today via the existing, unmodified `PricingService.calculateFinalFare` → `RideFare` pipeline (`research.md` §1.8) — this is a genuinely separate value (`RideFare.platformCommission`, which continues to feed the _customer_-side ledger posting, unrelated to the driver's wallet) and confirming or changing it has no effect on `Ride.commissionAmount` (FR-024d). See `data-model.md` §3.3 for exactly how the two coexist without interfering.
- **Direct consequence for BD-1**: since there is no second calculation, the _only_ way `ride.commissionAmount` can exceed the wallet balance available at completion is if the balance itself changed after acceptance (e.g. an `ADMIN_DEBIT`, or a recharge refund) — never because the commission figure changed, since it cannot change once stored. BD-1 (reversed) treats exactly this situation as an exceptional invariant violation, not a routine shortfall to cap and record.
- **Direct consequence for a Commission Plan/rate change mid-ride**: if the company changes `PricingRule.commissionRatePct` (or activates a different rule) after a ride has already been accepted, that ride's `Ride.commissionAmount` is unaffected — it was fixed at acceptance, against whichever rule was pinned at that moment. Only rides accepted after the change use the new rate. This is the same "a ride is never processed under two different [values]" principle BD-5 already established for payment models, applied here to the commission amount itself.
- **What is explicitly not introduced by this decision**: no driver debt/receivable system (none exists today, per `decisions.md` BD-1's original terms and this instruction's own reminder not to invent one), no second Commission Plan mechanism (BD-6 stands unchanged — this decision governs _when_ the existing mechanism is consulted, not _what_ it is), and no reservation/freeze (BD-4 stands unchanged — a database row lock at the moment of deduction remains ordinary write-safety, not a business reservation, and is unaffected by whether the amount being deducted was itself calculated moments ago or read from a value stored earlier).

## TD-1 — Interaction with existing settlement-time commission netting · **Unchanged**

Carried forward verbatim from the original decisions: `SettlementRepository`'s recovered-commission query is extended so commission collected via the Commission Wallet is never also netted at settlement. This decision is orthogonal to whether a reservation exists — it concerns settlement's interaction with the _fact_ that a wallet deduction occurred, not the mechanism (reservation-then-deduct vs. direct-deduct) by which it occurred. No change needed to this decision's text; it applies identically under the dual-model design, scoped to commission-model rides only (a subscription-model ride never produces a wallet deduction for TD-1 to need to exclude in the first place).

| `DRIVER_COMMISSION_WALLET_MAX_RECHARGE`            | New, operational           | Maximum for a **custom** recharge amount — new, needed now that custom-amount validation is an explicit functional requirement (FR-008a); predefined `WalletRechargeOption`s are validated against the configured option list instead, not this range. |
| ~~`DRIVER_COMMISSION_WALLET_RESERVATION_TTL_SEC`~~ | BD-4                       | **Removed** — nothing to expire, since nothing is ever reserved.                                                                                                                                                                                       |

None of these introduce a business policy beyond what BD-1 through BD-7 already settled. No knob is introduced for a minimum or maximum commission (BD-6, items 10–11) — none exists today and this feature adds none; if the product owner later wants one, that is new scope with its own configuration, not part of this feature.

## Open items — do not block implementation

Per BD-6, items 10–11 (minimum/maximum commission) are the only two of the fourteen Commission Plan questions without an existing-system answer. They are **not unresolved blockers**: "no minimum, no maximum" is the accurately-preserved current behavior (`PricingRule` defines no such fields), not a guess standing in for a missing answer. They are listed here only so that, if the product owner ever _wants_ to introduce a minimum or maximum commission as new functionality, that request is tracked as its own future decision rather than silently folded into this feature.
